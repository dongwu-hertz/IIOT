Safety & Intended Use
Read this chapter before installation, commissioning, field testing, or upgrade work.
Intended use
HZ-FA-371 is an online acoustic imaging camera intended for non-contact localization and analysis of sound sources in industrial environments. The supplied documents define three principal application modes: partial-discharge detection, pressurized-gas leak detection, and mechanical abnormal-noise detection.
Partial Discharge Detection. Captures ultrasonic discharge signals from electrical equipment and supports PRPD-based analysis and discharge-type identification.
Gas Leakage Detection. Locates turbulent acoustic emissions from pressurized leaks. The acoustic localization principle is not limited to a specific gas species; leakage estimation depends on configured site parameters.
Mechanical Abnormal-Noise Detection. Localizes friction, impact, and abnormal sound from rotating, reciprocating, and transmission equipment such as motors, pumps, fans, bearings, gearboxes, conveyors, compressors, and engines.
General safety
Follow all site safety rules, electrical clearances, hazardous-area procedures, and permit-to-work requirements.
Use appropriate PPE. Where toxic or hazardous gas may be present, use the respiratory and gas-detection protection required by the site.
Do not rely on the acoustic camera as the sole protective or shutdown system for personnel safety or process safety.
Keep the front microphone array and optical field of view unobstructed. Avoid severe vibration, impact, contamination, and mounting instability.
Use network, power, and mounting accessories suitable for the installation environment and the actual device nameplate.

After upgrading, verify software/FPGA versions, acoustic configuration, live view, alarm strategy, and real-time detection before returning the device to unattended service.
Product Overview
128-channel acoustic imaging with visible-light video and embedded condition analytics.
Acoustic imaging principle
The HZ-FA-371 uses a microphone array and beamforming-based localization. By comparing phase and timing differences between microphones, the device estimates sound-source direction and intensity and overlays the acoustic result on the visible-light image. The selected imaging frequency band determines which portion of the sound spectrum contributes to localization.

Installation position
Keep the front microphone array and visible-light camera unobstructed.
Minimize vibration transmitted from robots, pan-tilt units, vehicles, and machinery to the camera body.

Imaging threshold
The threshold controls whether a source is strong enough to be visualized. In the practical guide, -30 is given as a general starting point for imaging, but it is not a universal alarm threshold. In noisy industrial environments, increase the threshold until persistent background interference is suppressed while the target source remains visible.

Application Modes & Field Practice
Start with spectral observation, then tune band, threshold, alarm features, and detection period for the actual site.
Detection distance planning
The practical guide states that the best overall measurement performance is generally achieved within 1–20 m. Maximum usable distance is not fixed because acoustic energy is affected by source strength, frequency, air absorption, wind, background noise, obstacles, reflections, and scattering. As a rule of acoustic propagation, sound pressure decreases by approximately 6 dB when distance doubles under free-field geometric spreading.
[TABLE 14] rows=4
Range | Starting frequency guidance | Use
Near: 0.1–2 m | 2–65 kHz | Highest spatial resolution; tiny leaks and precise localization.
Medium: 2–20 m | 2–45 kHz | Best balance of distance and accuracy; routine PD/leak/mechanical inspection.
Long: 20–100 m | 2–30 kHz | Wide-area screening; low-frequency components propagate farther but spatial separation may decrease.

### Recommended operating sequence
1. Survey the spectrum  Observe the live spectrum with normal equipment operating.
2. Select the band  Choose a band that captures the discharge signature while avoiding dominant background noise.
3. Set threshold  Confirm a suspected discharge source produces stable acoustic imaging.
4. Run Single Detection  Collect repeated records and review PRPD and discharge-type feature behavior.
5. Build the alarm strategy  Combine frequency-domain and discharge-type conditions appropriate to the site.
6. Validate normal vs abnormal  Do not enable unattended Smart Patrol until false-alarm behavior is understood.
7. Enable Smart Patrol  Use periodic monitoring after thresholds and baseline behavior are confirmed.

Alarm Strategy & Decision Logic
Use acoustic localization plus one or more acoustic feature conditions to decide whether an event should be recorded as an alarm.
Strategy model
Only one alarm strategy can be enabled at a time. A strategy contains a strategy name, one or more alarm-condition rules, a detection period, strategy sensitivity, alarm level, and diagnostic notes. An enabled strategy or an alarm condition bound to an enabled strategy cannot be modified or deleted until it is disabled.
Figure 8-1. Alarm decision flow from the practical guide.
Feature definitions
[TABLE 19] rows=8
Feature | Meaning / use | Mode notes
Sound Pressure Level (SPL) | Sound-intensity level in dB. Lp = 20 log10(prms / pref), with pref = 20 µPa. | General
Dominant Frequency | Frequency component with the highest concentration / peak in the analyzed spectrum. | General; V2.0 supports selected analysis band.
Spectral Comparison | Compares the measured spectrum with a reference spectrum. Smaller difference means a closer match in the supplied strategy document. | General; requires reference file; V2.0 supports selected analysis band.
Band Energy Ratio (BER) | Energy within a selected frequency band divided by total signal energy. | V2.0 new alarm condition.
Discharge Type | Classification of discharge behavior; supplied documents refer to corona/point, floating, and surface discharge labels. | Partial Discharge only.
Kurtosis | Statistical measure sensitive to impulsive/heavy-tailed behavior. Normal steady machinery is described near 3 in the source guide; impacts can raise it. | Mechanical only.
Crest Factor | Signal peak divided by RMS value; higher values indicate stronger sharp peaks relative to average energy. | Mechanical only.

POST /login returns a token. All subsequent HTTP requests must include the token in the Authorization header. The API guide specifies the password hashing rule as MD5(plaintext password + "lfxs"), output as 32 lowercase hexadecimal characters. Invalid or missing authentication returns 401.

V2.0 API compatibility
Core HTTP request/response structures remain compatible with the previous platform according to the V2.0.11 release notes and version workbook.
V2.0 alarm-condition endpoints add fields including detect_mode, df_thr2, hfr_main_sub_freq_upper, and hfr_main_sub_freq_lower.
/system/leakage adds correction_rate in V2.0 and requires Leakage mode for the leakage-setting update.
V2.0 applies stricter validation to several endpoints, so invalid values that may previously have been accepted can now return an error.
Existing real-time WebSocket data channels remain structurally unchanged between the compared legacy and V2.0 versions.

Detection result timing
In Single Detection mode, POST /turn_on_detect returns a record ID. After detection completes, analysis and file saving may still require processing time. The API guide recommends waiting approximately 1–10 seconds before requesting GET /detect_logs/{detect_id}, with the required delay depending on the strategy period and system load.
Integration error handling
[TABLE 34] rows=8
Condition | Handling
401 authentication failure | Call POST /login again and retry with the new token.
WebSocket disconnect | Reconnect and resubscribe; ensure heartbeat is sent at least once per minute.
IP / port changed | Reconnect with the new address/port and update all clients.
Strategy locked | Disable the active strategy before modifying or deleting it or its bound condition.
No detection record yet | Wait for analysis/save completion, then retry GET /detect_logs/{detect_id}.

Troubleshooting & Maintenance
Use symptoms, live spectrum, device status, and logs to distinguish configuration problems from signal/environment problems.
[TABLE 35] rows=11
Symptom | Likely cause | Action
Cannot open Web page | PC/device not on same subnet; wrong IP/port; network issue. | Ping/reachability check, confirm 192.168.2.168 default only if unchanged, verify configured HTTP port.
No live video | Network/video service or browser connection problem. | Check LAN, refresh login, verify video settings and service health.
No acoustic image | Band does not include target; threshold too high; source too weak/far. | Inspect spectrum, move closer, select target band, lower threshold, confirm microphone self-test.
Frequent false alarms | Threshold too low, sensitivity too high, strong interfering band, reference file not representative. | Raise imaging/alarm thresholds, narrow feature band, reduce sensitivity, update comparison baseline.
Strategy cannot be edited | Strategy is enabled or condition is bound to enabled strategy. | Disable the strategy first.
No new Smart Patrol records | No alarm condition was triggered. | Verify enabled strategy and conditions; use Single Detection for diagnosis.
Records disappeared | Cyclic retention exceeded. | Export/archive externally; API guide states latest 50 records are retained.
WebSocket disconnects | Heartbeat missing or network interrupted. | Send heartbeat at least once per minute; reconnect and resubscribe.
Detection result not immediately available | Analysis/save still running. | Wait, then retry log/result retrieval.
Unexpected behavior after upgrade | Wrong package variant or settings not verified. | Check version/sampling compatibility, configuration, service logs, and FPGA status.
[/TABLE 35]
Preventive maintenance
Keep microphone openings and camera window clean and unobstructed using methods compatible with the installation environment.
Periodically confirm mounting rigidity and cable/connector condition.
Review device time, network settings, storage/record retention, and logs.
Use Device Self-Test to confirm microphone hardware status after maintenance or suspected impact.
Retain a record of site-specific imaging bands, alarm thresholds, strategy parameters, and reference spectra so settings can be restored after service.
Before/after software upgrades, record software version, FPGA version, and acoustic acquisition variant.
